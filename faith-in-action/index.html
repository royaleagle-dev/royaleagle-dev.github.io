The Reality

Imagine having land — but no tools to cultivate it.

In many remote African communities, families wake up every day facing challenges most of us rarely consider. Clean drinking water may be miles away. Parents may have land but lack the seeds, tools, irrigation, and farming knowledge needed to feed their families and generate income.

Children may go to bed hungry. Communities may have the desire to change their future but lack the resources to make that change possible. That is why sustainable agriculture sits at the heart of our work abroad: equipping farmers with seeds, tools, training, and market pathways so harvest becomes lasting security.

Closer to home, as a ministry based and incorporated in Texas, we see families facing different but equally urgent needs — empty pantries, unstable housing, educational gaps, and the hidden crisis of domestic violence. We serve here too: operating food pantries, supporting housing and schooling, running programs for children, campaigning against domestic violence, and walking alongside single mothers with practical help and dignity.

We believe poverty and oppression should never determine a person's future. Our mission is not simply to give — our mission is to empower.

Why We Serve

Our faith calls us to serve.

Jesus taught us to care for the hungry, the thirsty, the poor, and the oppressed. That calling is at the heart of everything we do — whether equipping farmers in Africa or standing with single mothers and survivors of domestic violence here in Texas.

We work alongside churches, community leaders, families and partners to bring practical solutions: clean water, seeds and tools, food pantries, housing and school support, kids programs, and advocacy that protects the vulnerable — while demonstrating the love, dignity and compassion of Christ.

Meet Amara

From surviving to feeding her village.

Amara lives with her husband and four children in a rural farming community in central Uganda. Her family had access to land, but for years they lacked the quality seeds, tools, and training needed to make it productive.

Through a partnership with the local church, Amara received a starter package of seeds, basic tools, and hands-on agricultural training. Today, her small plot produces enough maize and vegetables to feed her family — with surplus to sell at the local market.

"Now I teach my neighbors what I learned. We are not waiting to be helped — we are growing together."
